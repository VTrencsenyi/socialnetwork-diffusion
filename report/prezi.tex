        \item Agents are \hl{households}; the individual

              We run on the \hl{union network}.
        \item Also dropped: redundant, high-missingness, redacted, near-constant

      \end{itemize}

% 2 -- The two games
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{Two decision-theoretic games}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Transmission $G^T=\langle A,S,P_i,U_i\rangle$}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{Will this household inform the neighbour?}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item $A=\{\text{YES},\ \text{NO}\}$
          \item $S=\{\text{THEY JOIN},\ \text{THEY DON'T JOIN}\}$
          \item $U_i$: subjective risk/reward;\ \ $P_i$: beliefs over $S$
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
      \scriptsize
      Played by every \texttt{informed} household, each turn, for each
      not-yet-informed / not-yet-adopted neighbour $j\in N$.
      \smallskip

      Assumption: transmission is \hl{not a neutral act}. Consequences resemble a
      coordination game --- \emph{if I joined and told them, and they join too, I
      gain}.
      \smallskip

      \srcnote{Wider scope: an action space with intent ---
        \textsc{endorse} / \textsc{inform} / \textsc{oppose} / \textsc{ignore}.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Adoption $G^A=\langle A,S,P_i,U_i\rangle$}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{Will this household adopt microfinance?}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item $A=\{\text{YES},\ \text{NO}\}$
          \item $S=\{\text{BENEFICIAL},\ \text{LIMITED},\ \text{HARMFUL}\}$
          \item $U_i$: subjective risk/reward;\ \ $P_i$: beliefs over $S$
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
      \scriptsize
      Played once, whenever a household becomes \texttt{informed}.
      \smallskip

      $S$ is the time-agnostic coarsening of what a loan does to a household's
      finances: positive (house improvement), neutral (balance transfer),
      negative (misuse, debt spiral).
      \smallskip

      \srcnote{BCDJ factorise diffusion into the same two conditionally
        independent decisions --- a 7-parameter logit and a 2-parameter
        Bernoulli. We substitute one factor at a time.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

% 3 -- Agent architecture
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{Agent architecture: a structured wrapper, not off-the-shelf agency}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
      \scriptsize
      \textbf{1. The loop I would build --- OODA}
      \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
        \item \hl{Observe} --- neighbour states, what was said, village context
        \item \hl{Orient} --- household profile, priors, in/out-group standing
        \item \hl{Decide} --- the game above, over an explicit utility structure
        \item \hl{Act} --- adopt, or tell a specific neighbour
      \end{itemize}
      Models cognitive \emph{and} physical features and carries environmental
      uncertainty explicitly. Strongly structured architectures (old-school
      Wooldridge) \hl{wrapping} LLMs, rather than assuming inherent agency.
      \medskip

      \textbf{2. The loop actually implemented}
      \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
        \item \hl{Receive} --- one transmission is delivered
        \item \hl{Decide} --- one LLM call, one decision
        \item \hl{Advance} --- state update, next round
      \end{itemize}
      Loosely OODA-shaped, but sequential and stateless. Full OODA was cut for
      time and complexity, not because it is the wrong model.
      \medskip

      \srcnote{Simplifying assumptions taken from the original authors to save
        runtime: agents are informed once, decide once, and process one
        transmission at a time.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \centering
      % --- drop the OODA figure in here (e.g. ooda.png in this directory) ---

% 4 -- Three axes
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{What varies: three axes}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{1. Information set}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{What the agent is told.}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item Own profile (A)
          \item Neighbour's profile (B)
          \item Endorsement (C) / leader (L)
        \end{itemize}
        Each at three levels of richness: absent, listed facts, or an LLM-written
        narrative fixed per household id.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{2. Instruction design}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{How the agent is asked to reason.}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item Plain (D0)
          \item Model of appropriateness (D1)
          \item Decision-theoretic (D2)
        \end{itemize}
        Drawn from cognitive theory and decision theory --- not prompt
        engineering folklore.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{3. LLM choice}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{Who is answering.}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item \texttt{gpt-4.1-nano}, \texttt{gpt-5-nano}
          \item \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} (primary)
          \item \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna}
          \item \texttt{grok-4.20}, \texttt{grok-4.1-fast}
        \end{itemize}
        Non-reasoning models, so hidden processes do not confound the
        prompt-design effect.
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \srcnote{\texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} is the primary agent: cheapest recent SOTA API
    model, fast, and in game-theoretic work smaller models tend to behave more
    human-like. Claude is absent only because it does not accept the same strict
    JSON schema through the OpenAI-compatible API.}
\end{frame}

% 5 -- Prompt design mapping
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{The prompt design grid: A\,B\,C/L\,D}
  \centering\scriptsize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Axis} & \textbf{0} & \textbf{1} & \textbf{2} \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{A} --- ego profile     & no household info      & data as a listing        & LLM-written narrative \\
    \textbf{B} --- neighbour profile & no neighbour info    & data as a listing        & LLM-written narrative \\
    \textbf{C} --- endorsement \emph{(adoption)}   & decision not mentioned & neighbour's decision given & --- \\
    \textbf{L} --- leader \emph{(transmission)}    & not a seed             & seeded by the MFI          & --- \\
    \textbf{D} --- response format & plain                  & model of appropriateness & decision-theoretic \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

  \vfill
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \scriptsize
      \textbf{D1 --- model of appropriateness.} The agent answers, in order:
      \begin{enumerate}\scriptsize
        \item What kind of situation is this?
        \item What kind of person am I?
        \item What should someone like me do in a situation like this?
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \scriptsize
      \textbf{D2 --- decision-theoretic.} The agent must return a strict JSON
      schema populating the decision matrix of $G^A$/$G^T$ --- $P_i$ over $S$,
      $U_i$ over $A\times S$ --- \hl{with a justification per cell}, then choose.
      \smallskip

      \srcnote{This is what makes the reasoning auditable: the decision can be
        checked against the agent's \emph{own} matrix.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \srcnote{Adoption: $3\times3\times2\times3 = 54$ designs. Transmission: the
    same grid with C replaced by the conditional L.}
\end{frame}

% 6 -- Three experiments
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{Three experiments, increasing autonomy}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Pilot}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{Which prompt design is worth paying for?}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item No simulation loop --- two hand-built contrastive samples from
                village 6: one real adopter, one real non-adopter.
          \item Full factorial, both games, $n=25$ reps/arm.
          \item Fisher's exact per design, FDR-corrected.
          \item Plus: do D2 designs \hl{obey their own matrix}?
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Hybrid}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{Does an LLM beat a fitted logit at take-up?}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item BCDJ's Bernoulli transmission at $q_N,q_P$, transliterated
                unchanged.
          \item \hl{Only adoption is an LLM call} --- that single substitution is
                the whole experiment, which is what licenses attributing any gap
                to it.
          \item Mirror arm (LLM transmission, algorithmic adoption):
                \texttt{NotImplementedError}.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Full LLM}
        \scriptsize
        \emph{Can an ABM with zero fitted parameters reach a 2-parameter
        structural model?}
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item Adoption \hl{and} per-edge transmission are both LLM calls.
          \item Seeded only at the empirical leaders; BCDJ's timing kept.
          \item Scored against real take-up \emph{and} BCDJ's logit on the same
                network.
          \item Six models, two villages.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Hybrid --- incomplete, and instructive}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \fig{hybrid/adoption_by_design.png}{0.39}\\[0.3em]
      \fig{hybrid/household_auc.png}{0.36}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.43\textwidth}
      \scriptsize
      \textbf{The failure, plainly.} All 8 designs overshoot village 6's real
      take-up by \hl{2.0--4.0$\times$} (23.4\% $\to$ 46--94\%); BCDJ's logit sits
      at 18.9\%. Nor is it a ranking problem behind a level problem:
      \hl{AUC is 0.50--0.64}, only 1 of 8 keeps its interval above chance, and
      \hl{8 of 8} put leaders above non-leaders where the truth is 13.6\% against
      25.9\%.
      \smallskip

      \textbf{Where the divergence may come from}
      \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
        \item The pilot scored a \emph{contrast} between two curated samples; the
              loop demands an absolute \emph{level}, and nothing calibrates it.
        \item The pilot separated mostly through endorsement (C); under BCDJ's
              Bernoulli flood almost everyone is informed, so C fires everywhere
              and stops discriminating.
        \item Mechanism transfer is not free: context-sampling-to-LLM and
              LLM-to-LLM behave differently from
              \hl{algorithmic-transmission-to-LLM}.
        \item The mirror arm was never run, so the ablation that would localise
              this is missing.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

      status, not sensitivity to the village.

      a 17.6\% truth, everything newer at 54--88\%.

% 12 -- What an LLM buys
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{What could an LLM actually buy us here?}
  \begin{block}{1. Mechanism, not just fit}
    \scriptsize
    A calibrated, verified agent produces a reasoning trace that is a
    \emph{plausible} account of why this household joined --- the kind of
    explanation a human would give in the same circumstances. Two fitted numbers,
    $q_N$ and $q_P$, cannot say anything about why.
  \end{block}
  \begin{block}{2. Latent heterogeneity the covariates do not carry}
    \scriptsize
    The best single covariate explains $R^2=0.009$ of adoption. If social
    context, in/out-group standing and who-told-you matter, an LLM is one of the
    few ways to enter them into the model at all --- and it can generate
    counterfactual populations, not merely re-fit the observed one.
  \end{block}
  \begin{block}{3. Portability without a fitted parameter}
    \scriptsize
    The full-LLM model has \hl{zero fitted parameters}: it ran on village 73
    untouched. A structural model must be re-estimated per setting --- that is
    the trade this study is pricing.
  \end{block}
  \vfill
  \srcnote{\textbf{Not yet demonstrated.} Current results show sensitivity to
    social context but \hl{no advantage over the simpler model}: levels overshoot
    2--4$\times$, household ranking is near chance, the leader effect is a prior
    rather than a reading of the data, and D2 traces contradict their own
    matrices 30--45\% of the time. Stronger claims need reasoning ground truth.}
\end{frame}

% 13 -- Stretch
% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{Stretch target: a Western comparison population}
  \scriptsize
  The structure this study needs is \hl{demographics $+$ network $+$ observed
  adoption}. For a WEIRD population that combination is essentially unavailable
  off the shelf --- but experimental economics has close cousins, where the
  demographics are collected and ``adoption'' is a recorded choice under a known
  incentive structure.
  \smallskip

  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Candidate paradigms}
        \scriptsize
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item \textbf{Public goods games} --- contribution and conditional
                cooperation; the nearest analogue to take-up under social
                influence.
          \item \textbf{Sequential / multi-stage games} --- trust, centipede,
                bargaining: the same decision-under-a-signal shape as $G^A$.
          \item \textbf{Market entry games} --- coordination with a congestion
                externality, close to $G^T$.
          \item \textbf{Signalling games} --- for the endorsement channel (C)
                that drove the pilot separation.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What transfers, what does not}
        \scriptsize
        \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
          \item \hl{Transfers:} the agent architecture, the prompt-design grid,
                the D2 self-consistency audit, the whole scoring apparatus.
          \item \hl{Does not:} the real social network. Lab populations are
                anonymous and randomly rematched --- the topology would have to
                be commissioned, or recovered from a field experiment.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
      \scriptsize
      \textbf{Centaur} (Binz et al., 2024) is the proof the direction is
      tractable: one model fine-tuned across 160 experiments, predicting held-out
      human behaviour including in \emph{unseen} paradigms. The natural benchmark
      --- and a natural source of the behavioural ground truth this study lacks.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}
